I've been making things my whole life. Some of them were useful. Most of them weren't. All of them taught me something.

There's a version of creativity that's transactional—you make the thing, someone pays you for it or validates it, and that's the measure of success. I operated that way for a long time. I was good at it. I don't think it made me happy.

The version I'm more interested in now is the one where you make the thing because the making itself does something to you. Woodworking is the obvious example. Nobody *needs* another wooden cutting board. But there's something that happens in the hours of hand-sanding, of fitting joints, of standing back and looking at a thing that didn't exist this morning—something that doesn't happen anywhere else.

Writing is like that too, which is partly why this blog exists.

## The thing nobody tells you

Most things you make will be mediocre. Not because you're bad at making things—because that's how it works. Everything is practice for everything else. The cutting board I made last year is worse than the one I made last month. Both of them are practice for the one I haven't made yet.

I think we talk too much about the final product and not enough about the accumulation of bad versions that leads there.

## Why I'm sharing these things

I built this site partly because I wanted a place to catalog the music I'm living in—the Mixtapes section—and partly because I wanted a low-stakes place to write. No audience expectations. No algorithm. Just a place where things exist.

If you're reading this: hello. I hope you're making something too.
